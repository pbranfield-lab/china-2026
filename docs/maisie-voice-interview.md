# The Maisie Voice Interview

**What this is for:** the writing on this website is supposed to sound like Maisie. Right now it sounds like someone's *impression* of an eleven-year-old, which is not the same thing. These questions are here to capture how she actually talks, so the site can be rewritten in her real voice.

**How to use it:** give it straight to Maisie. Short answers are fine — a sentence or two each. There are no wrong answers and nothing here is a test.

**Important:** do not correct, tidy, or improve her answers. Keep her actual words, her spelling, her punctuation, the bits that trail off. The messy version is the useful version — a polished answer is exactly the thing we're trying to get away from.

Write answers under each question, or on paper, or say them out loud and have someone type them. Doesn't matter.

---

## Part 1 — Warm up

1. If you had to describe yourself in one sentence to someone who's never met you, what would you say?

2. What's a word or phrase you say ALL the time — the thing people would recognise as "so you" if they heard it?

3. What do you say when you're bored? What about when something's actually impressive?

4. Is there a word you're obsessed with right now? Is there one you think is really overused and annoying?

5. What's your favourite thing to complain about, and how do you usually complain about it?

6. What are you into at the moment — shows, games, YouTubers, music, anything — that you'd naturally bring up or compare stuff to?

---

## Part 2 — The family, in your words

7. Describe Mum in three sentences, the way you actually would to a friend. Not the nice version — the true one.

8. Describe Dad in three sentences.

9. Describe William in three sentences.

10. What's a real thing one of them says or does that winds you up? What about one that makes you laugh?

11. Do you have actual nicknames for them? What do you call them when you're annoyed, and when you're being nice?

12. Whose side are you usually on when Mum and William argue — and how do you let everyone know that?

---

## Part 3 — Tell it your way

For these, answer like you're texting a friend about what happened. A sentence or two is plenty. Don't overthink it, don't make it neat.

13. You queued for ages in the heat to get into somewhere. Tell me about it.

14. Someone — a guide, a security guard, whoever — told Mum "no" about something. What happened, and how did she take it?

15. William said something dry and sarcastic that actually landed. What would that sound like?

16. Dad missed a moment because he was faffing about with his camera. What's that like?

17. You just learned a genuinely wild fact about history — thousands of people building one thing, or something quite brutal that used to happen somewhere. React to it the way you actually would.

18. You finally got shade, or a sit down, or ice cream, after ages of walking. Describe the relief.

19. You saw something that was obviously built to show off — a massive building, a really fancy roof, a rare treasure in a case. What's your honest reaction?

20. Something turned out to be a bit sad, or heavy — like standing somewhere that something bad actually happened. How do you handle a serious moment? Do you make a joke, go quiet, or does it properly get to you?

---

## Part 4 — How you write

21. Do you use exclamation marks a lot when you write, or not really?

22. Do you use emoji when texting? Which ones do you actually use?

23. Is there slang you genuinely use that adults always get wrong when they try to write "a kid"?

24. What's something a made-up kid character says in a book or a show that makes you cringe?

25. Puns and wordplay — do you love them or find them annoying?

26. When you tell a story, do you go in order, or do you jump around and circle back to things?

---

## Part 5 — Quick ones

One line each. No explaining.

27. Something you'd 100% wear.

28. Something you would never, ever do.

29. A compliment you'd actually give something.

30. An insult you'd actually use. Nothing serious — "that's so mid" level.

31. How you'd end a story if you wanted to wrap it up.

---

## Notes for whoever does the rewrite

Part 3 is the important one. Those eight prompts match the situations that come up over and over across the site — the queue, Mum being told no, William's one-liners, Dad missing the shot, a wild historical fact, the relief of shade, something built to show off, and the heavy ending at the north gate. Her answers there are the reference material for rewriting the `story`, `william`, `caption` and `detail` fields in `assets/data.js`.

Part 2 matters because Mum-as-Queen-of-China, William-the-deadpan and Dad-behind-the-camera are running jokes the whole site leans on. If those characterisations aren't hers, no amount of vocabulary tweaking will make the writing sound right.

Parts 1, 4 and 5 are for vocabulary, rhythm and punctuation habits — the small stuff that makes a voice recognisable.
